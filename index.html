Theory of Collective Quantum Phases

Superconductivity · Quantum magnetism · Topological and disordered quantum matter

We study how interactions, topology, magnetism, disorder, and the surrounding environment shape collective quantum phases. Our work combines microscopic theory and computational modeling, with a particular interest in systems where interfaces and engineered structures provide new ways to control quantum behavior.

Research

We are interested in collective quantum phenomena and in how they can be controlled through interactions, interfaces, geometry, and external environments. Current directions span superconductivity, quantum magnetism and spintronics, topological phases, and disordered or amorphous quantum materials.

Superconductivity

Pairing mechanisms, unconventional superconductivity, interfaces, and engineered superconducting heterostructures.

Quantum magnetism

Spin transport, magnonics, superconducting spintronics, and coupled magnetic-superconducting systems.

Topology & disorder

Higher-order topology, topological superconductivity, amorphous quantum materials, and multifractal electronic states.
